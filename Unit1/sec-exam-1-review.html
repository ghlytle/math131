    1.

    \(2(2x+1) - (x-4) = 4(x+5)\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{gather*} 2(2x+1) - (x-4) = 4(x+5) \\ 4x+2 - x + 4 = 4x + 20\\ 3x + 6 = 4x + 20\\ 6 = x + 20\\ -14 = x \end{gather*}

    2.

    \(\displaystyle{-(2n-1)+3(n+2) = 7}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{gather*} -(2n-1)+3(n+2) = 7 \\ -2n + 1 + 3n + 6 = 7 \\ n + 7 = 7 \\ n = 0 \end{gather*}

    3.

    \(\displaystyle{\frac{2x+1}{3}+\frac{3x-1}{5} = \frac{1}{10}}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Identify the LCD: \(3 \cdot 5 \cdot 2 = 30\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Multiply both sides by the LCD:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{30}{1} \left(\frac{2x+1}{3} + \frac{3x-1}{5} \right) = \frac{30}{1}\left(\frac{1}{10}\right) \end{equation*}
    And distribute:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{30(2x+1)}{3} + \frac{30(3x-1)}{5} = \frac{30(1)}{10} \end{equation*}
    Now simplify:
    \begin{equation*} 10(2x+1) + 6(3x-1) = 3(1) \end{equation*}
    And solve the problem:
    \begin{gather*} 20x + 10 + 18x - 6 = 3\\ 38x + 4 = 3 \\ 38x = -1\\ x = -\frac{1}{38} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    \(\displaystyle{\frac{3n-1}{2}-\frac{2n+3}{7}=1}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Identify the LCD: \(2\cdot 7 = 14\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Multiply both sides by the LCD:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{14}{1}\left(\frac{3n-1}{2} - \frac{2n+3}{7}\right) = \frac{14}{1}\left(\frac{1}{1}\right) \end{equation*}
    And distribute:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{14(3n-1)}{2} - \frac{14(2n+3)}{7} = \frac{14(1)}{1} \end{equation*}
    Now simplify:
    \begin{equation*} 7(3n-1) - 2(2n+3) = 14(1) \end{equation*}
    And solve the problem:
    \begin{gather*} 21n - 7 - 4n - 6 = 14\\ 17n - 13 = 14 \\ 17n = 27\\ n = \frac{27}{17} \end{gather*}
    🔗

    5.

    \(\displaystyle{\frac{5x+6}{2}-\frac{x-4}{3} = \frac{5}{6}}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Identify the LCD: \(2 \cdot 3 = 6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Multiply both sides by the LCD:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{6}{1}\left(\frac{5x+6}{2} - \frac{x-4}{3}\right) = \frac{6}{1}\left(\frac{5}{6}\right) \end{equation*}
    And distribute:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{6(5x+6)}{2} - \frac{6(x-4)}{3} = \frac{6(5)}{6} \end{equation*}
    Now simplify:
    \begin{equation*} 3(5x+6) - 2(x-4) = 1(5) \end{equation*}
    And solve the problem:
    \begin{gather*} 15x+18 - 2x + 8 = 5\\ 13x + 26 = 5 \\ 13x = -21\\ x = -\frac{21}{13} \end{gather*}

    6.

    \(\displaystyle{1-\frac{2x-1}{6} = \frac{3x}{8}}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Identify the LCD: \(2\cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 24\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Multiply both sides by the LCD:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{24}{1}\left(\frac{1}{1} - \frac{2x-1}{6}\right) = \frac{24}{1}\left(\frac{3x}{8}\right) \end{equation*}
    And distribute:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{24(1)}{1} - \frac{24(2x-1)}{6} = \frac{24(3x)}{8} \end{equation*}
    Now simplify:
    \begin{equation*} 24 - 4(2x-1) = 3(3x) \end{equation*}
    And solve the problem:
    \begin{gather*} 24-8x+4 = 9x\\ 28 - 8x = 9x \\ 28 = 17x\\ \frac{28}{17} = x \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗

    7.

    \(\displaystyle{0.06x+0.08(x+100)=15}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Pick the power of 10 that we’re going to multiply: \(10^2 = 100\)
    🔗
    Multiply both sides of the equation by 100:
    \begin{equation*} 100(0.06x + 0.08(x+100)) = 100(15) \end{equation*}
    Now distribute:
    \begin{equation*} 100(0.06x) + 100(0.08)(x+100) = 100(15) \end{equation*}
    And simplify and solve:
    \begin{gather*} 6x + 8(x+100) = 1500\\ 6x + 8x + 800 = 1500\\ 14x + 800 = 1500\\ 14x = 700\\ x = 50 \end{gather*}
    🔗

    8.

    \(\displaystyle{0.2(x-0.5)-0.3(x+1) = 0.4}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Pick the power of 10 that we’re going to multiply: \(10^1 = 10\)
    🔗
    Multiply both sides of the equation by 10:
    \begin{equation*} 10\left(0.2(x-0.5) - 0.3(x+1)\right) = 10(0.4) \end{equation*}
    Now distribute:
    \begin{equation*} 10(0.2)(x-0.5) - 10(0.3)(x+1) = 10(0.4) \end{equation*}
    And simplify and solve:
    \begin{gather*} 2(x-0.5) - 3(x+1) = 4\\ 2x - 1 - 3x - 3 = 4\\ -x-4 = 4\\ -x = 8\\ x = -8 \end{gather*}

    11.

    \(\displaystyle{\frac{5}{6}n - \frac{1}{3}n < \frac{1}{6}}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Identify the LCD: \(2\cdot 3 = 6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Multiply both sides by the LCD:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{6}{1}\left(\frac{5}{6}n - \frac{1}{3}n\right) < \frac{6}{1}\left(\frac{1}{6}\right) \end{equation*}
    And distribute:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{6(5n)}{6} - \frac{6(1n)}{3} < \frac{6(1)}{6} \end{equation*}
    Now simplify:
    \begin{equation*} 5n - 2n < 1 \end{equation*}
    And solve the problem:
    \begin{gather*} 3n < 1\\ n < \frac{1}{3} \end{gather*}

    12.

    \(\displaystyle{\frac{n-4}{5} + \frac{n-3}{6} > \frac{7}{15}}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Identify the LCD: \(5\cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 30\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Multiply both sides by the LCD:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{30}{1}\left(\frac{n-4}{5} + \frac{n-3}{6}\right) > \frac{30}{1}\left(\frac{7}{15}\right) \end{equation*}
    And distribute:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{30(n-4)}{5} + \frac{30(n-3)}{6} > \frac{30(7)}{15} \end{equation*}
    Now simplify:
    \begin{equation*} 6(n-4) + 5(n-3) > 2(7) \end{equation*}
    And solve the problem:
    \begin{gather*} 6n-24 + 5n-15 > 14\\ 11n - 39 > 14 \\ 11n > 53\\ n > \frac{53}{11} \end{gather*}

    13.

    Express the solutions in interval notation.
    🔗

    (a)

    \(x>-1\) and \(x\leq 1\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    First you plot the inequality \(x>-1\text{.}\) The shading begins at \(-1\) and ends at \(\infty\text{,}\) with an open circle at \(-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Next, you plot the inequality \(x \leq 1\text{.}\) The shading begins at \(-\infty\) and ends at 1, with a filled-in circle at 1.
    🔗
    Because the compound inequality is an and, we want the overlap between the two pictures. The overlapped shading starts at \(-1\) and ends at 1. But there is an open circle at \(-1\text{.}\) Thus the solution is the interval \((-1, 1]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    \(x<2\) or \(x>-1\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    First you plot the inequality \(x<2\text{.}\) The shading begins at \(-\infty\) and ends at \(2\text{,}\) with an open circle at \(2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Next, you plot the inequality \(x > -1\text{.}\) The shading begins at \(-1\) and ends at \(\infty\text{,}\) with a open circle at \(-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Because the compound inequality is an or, we want the regions where at least one line is shaded (meaning one of them or both of them). In that search, we note that the shading starts at \(-\infty\) and continues on toward \(\infty\text{.}\) The number \(2\) is excluded from the first inequality, but it is included in the second. The same thing happens with the value \(-1\text{.}\) Thus the solution for the inequality is the interval \((-\infty, \infty)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    \(x+1 > 3\) and \(x-3 < -5\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The first step is to solve both of the inequalities individually. We can solve these by getting \(x\) by itself.
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} x + 1 > 3 \\ x > 2 \end{gather*}
    \begin{gather*} x-3 < -5 \\ x < -2 \end{gather*}
    Next, we’ll plot the inequality \(x>2\text{.}\) The shading begins at \(2\) and ends at \(\infty\text{,}\) with an open circle at \(2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Then, we’ll plot the inequality \(x < -2 \text{.}\) The shading begins at \(-\infty\) and continues until \(-2 \text{,}\) with an open circle around \(-2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Now, when we look at the logical operator, we are seeking for the overlap between these graphs, and there is none! That means that there is NO SOLUTION to the compound inequality.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    \(4x-12 > 0\) or \(-3x+5 \geq 11\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The first step is to solve both of the inequalities individually. We can solve these by getting \(x\) by itself.
    🔗
    \begin{gather*} 4x - 12 > 0 \\ 4x > 12 \\ x > 3 \end{gather*}
    \begin{gather*} -3x+5 \geq 11 \\ -3x \geq 6 \\ x \leq -2 \end{gather*}
    Next, we plot the inequality \(x > 3\text{.}\) The shading begins at \(3\) and ends at \(\infty\text{,}\) with an open circle at \(3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    To plot the inequality \(x \leq -2 \text{,}\) the shading begins at \(-\infty\) and ends at \(-2\) with a filled-in circle at \(-2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Next we are dealing with an or operation. That means we look at where there is either one shading active or both active. In this particular problem, there are two disjoint regions, so the solution is the union \((-\infty, -2] \cup (3, \infty)\)

    14.

    How many cups of orange juice must be added to \(50\) cups of a punch that is \(10\%\) orange juice to obtain a punch that is 20%orange juice?
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is a mixing model problem. so we are working with the following model
    \begin{equation*} \binom{Initial}{Volume}\binom{Initial}{Concentration} + \binom{Added}{Concentration}\binom{Added}{Concentration} = \binom{End}{Concentration}\binom{End}{Volume} \end{equation*}
    We know that the initial concentration is \(0.10\) with an initial volume of \(50\text{.}\) Next, we are adding pure orange juice, so the concentration added is \(1\text{,}\) and the volume added is \(x\text{,}\) our unknown.
    🔗
    At the end of the mixing, we have a concentration of \(0.20\text{.}\) Note that the volume at the end is given by \((50+x)\text{.}\) That gives us the following algebraic equation to model the situation.
    \begin{equation*} (0.10)(50) + x = (0.20)(50+x) \end{equation*}
    We can then multiply both sides of the equation by 10 to get rid of the decimals.
    \begin{align*} 10\left((0.10)(50) + x\right) \amp = 10\left((0.2)(50+x)\right) \\ 50 + 10x \amp = 2(50+x)\\ 50 + 10x \amp = 100 + 2x\\ 50 + 8x \amp = 100\\ 8x \amp = 50\\ x \amp = \dfrac{25}{4} \end{align*}
    🔗

    15.

    How many gallons of a 12%-salt solution must be mixed with \(6\) gallons of a 20%-salt solution to obtain a 15%-salt solution?
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is a mixing model problem. so we are working with the following model
    \begin{equation*} \binom{Initial}{Volume}\binom{Initial}{Concentration} + \binom{Added}{Concentration}\binom{Added}{Concentration} = \binom{End}{Concentration}\binom{End}{Volume} \end{equation*}
    We know that the initial concentration is \(0.20\) with an initial volume of \(6\text{.}\) Next, the concentration added is \(0.12\text{,}\) and the volume added is \(x\text{,}\) our unknown.
    🔗
    At the end of the mixing, we have a concentration of \(0.15\text{.}\) Note that the volume at the end is given by \((6+x)\text{.}\) That gives us the following algebraic equation to model the situation.
    \begin{equation*} (0.20)(6) + (0.12)x = (0.15)(6+x) \end{equation*}
    We can then multiply both sides of the equation by 100 to get rid of the decimals.
    \begin{align*} 100\left((0.20)(6) + 0.12x\right) \amp = 100\left((0.15)(6+x)\right) \\ 20(6) + 12x \amp = 15(6+x) \\ 120 + 12x \amp = 90 + 15x \\ 120 \amp = 90 + 3x \\ 30 \amp = 3x\\ 10 \amp = x \end{align*}

    16.

    Lauren has scores of 52, 84, 65, and 74 on her first four math exams. What score must she make on the fifth exam to have an average of 70 or better for the five exams?
    🔗
    Solution.
    We first define a variable \(x\) to represent the score on the fifth exam. Then the average of the five exams is given by the expression
    \begin{equation*} \frac{52+84+65+74+x}{5}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Because we want the average to be \(70\) or better, we want to solve this inequality:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{52+84+65+74+x}{5} \geq 70\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    To solve this inequality, we first combine like terms in the numerator of the fraction.
    \begin{equation*} \frac{275+x}{5} \geq 70 \end{equation*}
    And now, we will multiply both sides of the inequality by the denominator 5 to get rid of the fraction.
    \begin{equation*} 275+x \geq 5\cdot 70 \end{equation*}
    We then solve the inequality for \(x\text{.}\)
    \begin{gather*} 275 + x \geq 350 \\ x \geq 75 \end{gather*}
    Thus Lauren must score a \(75\) or higher to earn an average of \(70\) or more.

    17.

    Sari had scores of 94, 84, 86, and 88 on her first four exams of the semester. What score must she obtain on the fifth exam to have an average of 90 or better for the five exams?
    🔗
    Solution.

    \begin{equation*} \frac{94+84+86+88+x}{5}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Because we want the average to be \(70\) or better, we want to solve this inequality:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{94+84+86+88+x}{5} \geq 90\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    To solve this inequality, we first combine like terms in the numerator of the fraction.
    \begin{equation*} \frac{352+x}{5} \geq 90 \end{equation*}
    And now, we will multiply both sides of the inequality by the denominator 5 to get rid of the fraction.
    \begin{equation*} 352+x \geq 5\cdot 90 \end{equation*}
    We then solve the inequality for \(x\text{.}\)
    \begin{gather*} 352 + x \geq 450 \\ x \geq 98 \end{gather*}
    Thus Sari must score a \(98\) or higher to earn an average of \(90\) or more.

    18.

    Judy bought a coat at a 20% discount sale for $72. What was the original price of the coat?
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is an example of a discount problem. Because the discount is given as a percentage of the original price, then the model looks like:
    \begin{equation*} \binom{\text{Original}}{\text{Price}} - \binom{\%}{\text{off}}\cdot\binom{\text{Original}}{\text{Price}} = \binom{\text{Sale}}{\text{Price}}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Let \(p\) represent the original price of the coat. We can substitute the information from the problem into our model to get:
    \begin{equation*} p - 0.20p = 72 \end{equation*}
    This is now an equation involving decimals. One way to solve it is to multiply both sides of the equation by the power of 10 that removes the decimal places from the equation. In this case, we will multiply both sides of the equation by 100 and then solve.
    \begin{align*} 100(p - 0.20p) \amp = 100(72)\\ 100p - 20p \amp = 7200 \\ 80p \amp = 7200\\ p \amp = 90 \end{align*}
    Thus the original price of the coat was $90.

    19.

    Jim bought a pair of jeans at a 25% discount sale for $45. What was the original price of the jeans?
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is an example of a discount problem. Because the discount is given as a percentage of the original price, then the model looks like:
    \begin{equation*} \binom{\text{Original}}{\text{Price}} - \binom{\%}{\text{off}}\cdot\binom{\text{Original}}{\text{Price}} = \binom{\text{Sale}}{\text{Price}}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Let \(p\) represent the original price of the jeans. We can substitute the information from the problem into our model to get:
    \begin{equation*} p - 0.25p = 45 \end{equation*}
    This is now an equation involving decimals. One way to solve it is to multiply both sides of the equation by the power of 10 that removes the decimal places from the equation. In this case, we will multiply both sides of the equation by 100 and then solve.
    \begin{align*} 100(p - 0.25p) \amp = 100(45)\\ 100p - 25p \amp = 4500 \\ 75p \amp = 4500\\ p \amp = 60 \end{align*}
    Thus the original price of the jeans was $60.
